# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Carbon Accounting Platform Development (Technical Leadership & Data Engineering)
**S:** Ducky AS needed a scalable carbon accounting platform to serve 70+ corporate clients. The existing manual process of calculating greenhouse gas footprints was time-consuming and error-prone.
**T:** Lead the development of the platform's core GHG accounting models and data infrastructure.
**A:** Engineered ETL pipelines integrating ERP system data with emission factor databases. Built greenhouse gas calculation models aligned with VSME and ESRS frameworks. Automated data ingestion from multiple client systems.
**R:** Delivered a production platform handling GHG accounting for 70+ corporate clients, with automated calculations aligned to regulatory frameworks.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a project you led", "Describe your experience with data engineering", "How do you ensure accuracy in your work"

### 2. NLP Model for GHG Classification (Machine Learning & Innovation)
**S:** Financial transaction data from clients lacked GHG Protocol Scope classification, making it difficult to automate carbon footprint calculations.
**T:** Develop a method to automatically classify transactions by GHG Scope and map them to appropriate emission factors.
**A:** Built a transformer-based NLP model that analyses financial transaction descriptions and assigns GHG Protocol Scope classifications. Integrated the model into the production pipeline to automatically match transactions with the correct emission factors.
**R:** Improved the accuracy and speed of greenhouse gas calculations, enabling fully automated carbon footprint reporting.
**Use for:** "Describe a machine learning model you built", "How do you apply AI to solve real-world problems", "Tell me about a time you improved a process"

### 3. Urban Mobility Analysis with Telecom Data (Data Analysis & Cross-Functional Work)
**S:** Municipalities needed data-driven insights on commuting patterns to support sustainable urban mobility planning, but traditional survey methods were limited.
**T:** Process and analyse regional telecommunication data to model local commuting patterns across multiple municipalities.
**A:** Cleaned and analysed large-scale anonymized telco mobility datasets. Applied statistical methods to identify commuting corridors and peak travel patterns. Translated findings into actionable insights for urban planners.
**R:** Delivered actionable data insights supporting sustainable urban mobility initiatives across 5+ municipalities.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you worked with a large dataset", "Describe a cross-functional project", "How do you turn data into actionable insights"

### 4. Lifecycle Assessment for Circular Economy (Environmental Modelling & Impact Analysis)
**S:** Ducky wanted to understand the environmental impact of different equipment ownership models (buying new vs. rental vs. reuse) to support circular economy initiatives.
**T:** Build a lifecycle assessment model to compare environmental impacts across scenarios.
**A:** Developed an LCA model quantifying greenhouse gas emissions across the full lifecycle of equipment. Collected and normalized data across usage scenarios. Ran sensitivity analyses on key variables.
**R:** Demonstrated a 25–30% reduction in lifecycle GHG emissions for reuse/rental scenarios, providing quantitative backing for circular business model decisions.
**Use for:** "Describe your experience with lifecycle assessment", "How do you approach environmental impact analysis", "Tell me about a time your analysis influenced business decisions"

### 5. Geospatial Modelling for Heat Pump Planning (Geospatial Analysis & Research)
**S:** German municipalities needed to identify optimal sites for heat pump installations as part of their heating transition strategies, but lacked systematic tools.
**T:** Build a geospatial model integrating multiple datasets to identify suitable installation sites.
**A:** Combined OpenStreetMap data with building, climate, and infrastructure datasets using Python. Developed a simulation model to estimate residential geothermal heating potential. Designed interactive PowerBI dashboards showing CO2 abatement costs and heat demand reduction.
**R:** Provided quantitative data to support municipal heating transition strategies across 5 German municipalities, enabling planners to identify cost-effective sites for geothermal energy deployment.
**Use for:** "Describe a research project you worked on", "How do you handle geospatial or spatial data", "Tell me about a time you presented data to non-technical stakeholders"

### 6. Thesis During COVID-19 (Resilience & Adaptability)
**S:** Master's thesis required conducting research at a German institute (Fraunhofer ISE) during the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic, with personal hardships arising from the situation back home in India.
**T:** Complete rigorous thesis research remotely, including developing a geothermal potential model for a German case study (Lörrach), while navigating language barriers (working in German without proficiency) and pandemic challenges.
**A:** Independently organized the research timeline, interpreted German-language literature despite not speaking the language, maintained communication with supervisors across countries (Germany and Sweden), and delivered high-quality work on schedule.
**R:** Produced a well-structured thesis ("Estimating the technical shallow geothermal potential for heating: A case study of Lörrach, Germany") praised by supervisors for its analytical depth and clarity, earned strong recommendation letters from both academic and industry supervisors.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you overcame a challenge", "How do you handle working under pressure", "Describe a time you had to adapt to difficult circumstances"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave your previous role / why are you looking?"
> I'm currently employed at Ducky AS as a Climate Footprint Analyst and have grown significantly here — leading the development of our carbon accounting platform and building ML models. I'm looking for the next step where I can apply my data and analytics skills in a slightly different context, and I'm particularly drawn to roles that combine quantitative analysis with strategic decision-making impact.

### "You don't have direct experience in [commodity markets / finance / specific domain]."
> That's correct — my background is in sustainability and energy systems rather than finance or commodity markets. However, the core analytical skills transfer directly: I build data pipelines, develop quantitative models, and turn complex datasets into clear insights for decision-makers. What I bring is a rigorous, data-driven approach to analysis, strong Python/SQL skills, and the ability to quickly learn new domains — as I've demonstrated working across energy systems, ESG, and geospatial analysis. I'm genuinely interested in this domain and have been following it closely.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> I want to grow as a data analyst who sits at the intersection of technical modelling and strategic decision-making. In five years, I'd like to be recognised as someone who can take complex data challenges — whether in sustainability, markets, or energy — and deliver insights that shape business strategy. I'm looking for a role where I can continue building my analytical toolkit while deepening my domain expertise.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> I sometimes dive deep into the technical details of a problem when a simpler solution might suffice earlier on. I've learned to counter this by setting clear scope boundaries upfront and checking in with stakeholders to make sure the level of detail I'm building toward is what's actually needed. This has helped me deliver faster without sacrificing quality.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the project lifecycle look like, from idea to delivery?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for data analysis and modelling?"
- "Is there room to grow into more strategic or architectural decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
